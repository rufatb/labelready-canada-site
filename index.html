02Free self-check

The label self-check — two minutes, no sign-up

Answer nine questions about a label you know well. The result lists the items most likely to be flagged in a full preflight. It reads your answers only — the paid preflight reads the artwork itself. Nothing is stored or sent; the answers stay in your browser.

1. If your product meets the thresholds for saturated fat, sugars or sodium, does the label carry the front-of-package nutrition symbol?
2. Is the Nutrition Facts table in the current standard format, including the % daily value footnote?
3. Are the ingredients listed in descending order by weight, with the components of prepared ingredients broken out where required?
4. Does the label carry a "Contains" statement that matches the priority allergens in the ingredient list?
5. If the label says "may contain", is that based on a cross-contact assessment by the manufacturer, rather than added just in case?
6. Is every mandatory item — ingredients, allergens, net quantity, storage directions — in both English and French?
7. Is the net quantity declared in metric units (for example, 355 mL) on the principal display panel?
8. Does the principal display panel show the product's common name, not only the brand name?
9. Does the label show the name and principal place of business of the responsible party, plus best-before and storage information where your product requires them?

04How it works

From artwork to report in four steps

  1. Submit the intake

    Create an account and answer the intake questions: product, net quantity, the exact ingredient statement, allergens, and the nutrition values as you will declare them. Nothing about your label is inferred or filled in for you.

  2. Send your artwork

    After intake, we send a private upload link for your print-ready files (front and back, PDF preferred). Artwork is used only to prepare your report and is deleted 30 days after delivery.

    $49 CAD by card, one SKU at a time. There is no subscription and nothing renews. If your product falls outside the supported scope, the eligibility check happens before any payment.

  4. Receive the report

    Within the standard turnaround of 2 business days you get the annotated report, the citations behind each finding, and a designer-ready fix list. You can follow the order from your dashboard at any point.

07FAQ

Questions, answered plainly

Is this a certification or legal advice?

No. A preflight is a structured check of your artwork against published CFIA and Health Canada requirements, with the source cited for every finding. It is not a CFIA certification, a legal opinion, or a guarantee that a regulator will accept the label. Findings that turn on interpretation are marked as needing expert review rather than passed.

How long does it take?

The standard turnaround is 2 business days from a complete submission — artwork plus the intake facts. If something is missing, we email you, and the clock restarts when it arrives.

What exactly do I send?

Your print-ready artwork (PDF preferred, front and back) and the facts in the intake form: the exact ingredient statement, declared allergens, nutrition values as they will appear, and the net quantity. We check what is on the label. We do not calculate nutrition values from a recipe.

My product is not a sauce or a condiment. Can I still use this?

The supported scope today is Ontario shelf-stable sauces and condiments. Anything else goes through a free eligibility check first. If we cannot check your category properly, we decline the order and no charge is taken.

What if the preflight finds problems?

That is a common result, including for labels already in market. You receive each finding, the rule it maps to, and suggested corrected wording where the fix is deterministic, so your designer can make the changes in one pass. We refund when we cannot complete a check, not when the check finds issues.

Who runs LabelReady Canada?

LabelReady Canada is a service of Banriq IQ, a sole proprietorship based in Richmond Hill, Ontario. Reports are prepared against the published federal requirements. Where a finding needs a qualified Canadian food-labelling specialist, the report says so instead of guessing.
